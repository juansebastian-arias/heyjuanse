# Product

Why the site is what it is. Written to be reviewed later, not to persuade.

## The brief

Show the capability instead of describing it. The site is for three readers at once: a recruiter
with seconds, a business leader who wants consequences in numbers, and an engineer who wants to
see whether the logic holds. It must not be a portfolio, a resume or a list of skills, and it must
never present a made-up figure as a result achieved somewhere.

## What it is

One page: a small company you can watch, question and fix. Customer requests move from first
message to paid invoice through five teams. The page says where buyers give up waiting, ranks the
changes that could be made, and shows what each is worth against what it costs. Everything under
it can be opened and checked.

## What was set aside, and why

Three earlier versions of this site were built and judged before this one.

| Version | What it showed | Why it was set aside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A lab of rules | A week of messy leads, rules to change, every metric a query in the browser. | Correct and deep, but a reader had to learn the tool before seeing any value. |
| A simulator with switches | Go-to-market strategies and automations over 90 days of leads. | Read as a calculator: a form to fill in, with a number at the end. The value was not visible until the reader had done work. |
| One lead, two ways | A request answered in 42 hours against one answered in minutes, then an AI agent. | Clear in seconds but narrow: one step of one process, and its headline figure came from someone else's study. |

The lesson from all three: the reader should see the whole thing working before deciding anything,
and the first action should be one click whose consequence is visible.

Other directions were weighed and dropped for the same reasons: a diagnostic the reader fills in
with their own company's numbers (asks for work first, and invites false precision), and a
gallery of separate small demos (no single thing to understand, and it reads as a portfolio).

## Why this one

- **It is understood before it is operated.** A company with a line of customers and a pile of
  the ones who gave up needs no instructions.
- **One model serves all three readers.** The recruiter presses one button and sees a result. The
  business reader gets the gain, the cost and the uncertainty of each change. The engineer can
  open the assumptions, rerun the validation and read the source.
- **It is not limited to sales.** Waiting, hand-offs, rework and capacity are the same problem in
  support, finance, onboarding and operations. Larkspur's five teams cover the path from request
  to cash on purpose.
- **It is honest by construction.** The company is labeled as made up above the headline. No
  figure is typed in by hand; each comes out of a simulation the reader can rerun.

## Decisions on the page

- **The move comes before the company.** The best next move and its button sit under the headline,
  above the floor, so the one thing to do is in view on a short laptop screen and on a phone. The
  company it acts on is directly under it.
- **The result is reported where the click was.** After a change, a sentence in the same console
  says what the changes so far came to. It names a figure only when that figure clearly moved.
- **One recommendation, not a menu.** The planner's best move is offered first. Every other change
  is still there to try, in the panel of its team.
- **Uncertainty is shown, not hidden.** Each figure carries the range it fell in for eight
  quarters in ten. A change that cannot be told from luck is called that.
- **Depth is one level down, never in the way.** Five layers under the main screen, each answering
  one question.
- **Nothing about the author until the end.** The footer says who built it and how to reach them.
  The site never says its author is looking for work.
- **The bridge to a real company is one sentence and a link**, to the five steps the same method
  takes with a company's own data.

## What it deliberately does not do

- It does not claim results. The dollar figures follow from stated assumptions.
- It does not take the reader's own numbers. That is the work itself, and a form on a web page
  would turn it into a guess with decimals.
- It does not use AI where arithmetic does the job. The model is used for one step that needs
  judgment, with a person approving, and the page says when a run is live and when it is a
  recording.
